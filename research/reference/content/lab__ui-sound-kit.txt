[y=1066 fs=32px] The idea
[y=1136 fs=17px] Interfaces are almost always silent, so the few sounds they do make tend to be loud and generic. I wanted a set that sits under the interaction instead of on top of it.
[y=1210 fs=17px] The brief to myself: 48 sounds, none longer than 120 ms, all of them pleasant on the hundredth play.
[y=1309 fs=32px] How it's built
[y=1380 fs=17px] Every sound is synthesised in the browser with the Web Audio API, so the kit has no audio files at all and each sound can be tuned with three numbers.
[y=1438 fs=17px] Oscillator, filter and envelope per sound, nothing else
[y=1475 fs=17px] Pitch follows the size of the element you touch
[y=1511 fs=17px] Respects reduced-motion and a global mute
[y=1548 fs=17px] Exported as a 2 kB module with a tiny play() function

[y=1990 fs=32px] What I learned
[y=2060 fs=17px] Quiet beats clever. The sounds people kept were the ones they stopped noticing after a day.
[y=2089 fs=17px] Short decays feel faster than short attacks
[y=2126 fs=17px] Always ship a mute that remembers itself
[y=2214 fs=24px] A good UI sound is one you only miss when it's gone.
