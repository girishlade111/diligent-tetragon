[y=1066 fs=32px] The idea
[y=1136 fs=17px] I kept rewriting the same easing curves from memory on every project. So I put the ones I trust on one page.
[y=1210 fs=17px] Every curve plays on a small card, with the exact values ready to copy for CSS, Framer and Swift.
[y=1309 fs=32px] How it's built
[y=1380 fs=17px] A Framer site with one component per curve. The cards are driven by a single data list, so adding a new curve is one line, and the copy button formats it for the tool you pick.
[y=1438 fs=17px] Twelve curves, each with a use case
[y=1475 fs=17px] Copy as CSS, Framer Motion or SwiftUI
[y=1511 fs=17px] Cards replay on hover so you can compare

[y=1918 fs=32px] What I learned
[y=1988 fs=17px] Most motion problems are timing problems. The right duration fixes more than the perfect curve.
[y=2017 fs=17px] Enter slower than you exit
[y=2054 fs=17px] Name curves after what they do, not their numbers
[y=2142 fs=24px] A shared cheatsheet ended more design-dev arguments than any spec I wrote.
